Welcome — here's how this works

This is a solo storytelling game. There is no opponent and no way to play badly. You invent a vampire, then write their life story across a thousand years, a few sentences at a time.

  1. Make your vampire. Answer eight short questions — who you were as a mortal, what you owned, who you loved, how you died. Stuck? Every question has a 🎲 button that suggests answers, and ✨ Surprise me fills in an entire vampire for you.
  2. Roll the dice. The dice move you through a book of 80 numbered Prompts. You don't choose where you land.
  3. Read the Prompt and write. Each one asks a question about your vampire's life — "who betrays you?", "what do you lose?". Answer it in a sentence or a paragraph, in your own words.
  4. Record what changed. A Prompt may tell you to gain a skill, lose something, or let someone die. Buttons on screen do that bookkeeping for you.
  5. Repeat until the story ends. A game runs anywhere from twenty minutes to many sittings. Everything saves automatically.

Centuries pass. Your vampire outlives everyone they knew, and can only hold five memories at a time — so as new ones arrive, old ones must be given up. That loss is the point of the game.

What the words mean

Prompt
One of the 80 numbered questions in the book. The dice decide which one you get. Each Prompt has three versions — a, b and c — so landing on the same number again gives you a new question rather than a repeat.
Experience
One sentence (or a short paragraph) about something that happened to your vampire. This is the thing you actually write.
Memory
A container holding up to three related Experiences, with a short Theme naming it — like "The Siege of Antioch". You can only keep five Memories. To take a new one you must forget an old one.
Theme
The title you give a Memory. A few words, nothing more.
Skill
Something your vampire is good at: "Swordplay", "Reading Latin". When a Prompt says check a Skill, tick one off — it's been used up or has failed you, and can't be checked again.
Resource
Something your vampire owns or controls: a house, a horse, a debt owed. Prompts will take these away.
Character
A person in your vampire's life. Mortals die of old age within a generation; Immortals (like the vampire who made you) do not.
Mark
A visible sign of what you are — no reflection, cold hands, a wound that never healed.
Lose / strike out
Crossing something off. It stays on the page with a line through it, as a record of what you used to have.
Diary
A safe place to store up to four Memories you don't want to forget forever. It's itself a Resource — if you lose the Diary, everything in it is lost too.
The Boxed Experience
One perfect moment you write once, at any time. It never fades and never takes up a Memory slot.
Checked Skill
A Skill with its box ticked: already spent. Unticked Skills are still available to you.
Memory states
Prompts can change how a Memory works — ⭐ Starred (safe forever, doesn't use a slot), 🌫️ Hazy (only fragments remain), 🌌 Vast (holds five Experiences), 🐾 Primal (only a feeling is left).
Doom dots
A countdown on an Immortal Character. One Prompt late in the book halves their remaining lifespan for each dot.
The Chronicle
Everything you've written, gathered in one place. Export or print it when you're done.

Step 1: Your Mortal Identity

Before you became a monster, you were a person. What was your name?

Step 2: Mortal Skills

A Skill is anything your vampire is good at. It doesn't have to be heroic — a trade, a knack, a way with people all count.

What were you exceptionally good at in your human life? List three. (e.g., "Blacksmithing", "Charming", "Spear Fighting", "Herbalism")

Step 3: Resources

A Resource is something you own or control. Later Prompts will take these away from you, one by one.

What valuable physical items or properties do you possess from your human years? List three. (e.g., "A fine horse", "A hidden cave", "Bag of gold coins", "Family signet ring")

Step 4: Mortal Characters

A Character is a person who matters to your vampire. They can be loved or hated — either works, and both hurt to lose.

Name three mortals intimately tied to your life — relatives, friends, lovers, enemies, mentors. (e.g., "Eliza, my sister", "Marcus, the local priest"). The immortal who turns you comes later, in Step 8.

Step 5: Memory 1 — Your Mortal Life

The next four steps are the writing part. A Memory is just a titled box holding a sentence or two — its title is the Theme, and each sentence inside is an Experience. Write plainly; nobody else has to read this.

Start your first Memory with one broad Experience summarizing who you were before unlife. (e.g. Theme: 'Mortal Life', Experience: 'I am Henri, son of Jon, a poor knight swindled out of my inheritance near the Loire.')

Step 6: Memories 2 & 3

Now tie your vampire's life together: each of these should mention two of the things you listed in steps 2–4 (a Skill, a Resource or a person). The panel below reminds you what you wrote.

Create two more Experiences, each in its own Memory, each combining two of your traits. (e.g. "Gundar takes me on my first voyage aboard the Longship Bøkesuden; his touch calms me when we first leave sight of land.")

Step 7: Memory 4

One more Experience in its own Memory, again combining two of your traits.

Step 8: The Turning

The last step: how you stopped being human. The vampire who did it to you is called your sire, and a Mark is the physical trace it left on you.

Lastly, create the Immortal who gifted (or cursed) you with unlife, the Mark it left, and the Memory of your transformation (Memory 5). (e.g. Sire: 'Baron Hollmueller, an Austrian vampire'; Mark: 'My neck is permanently broken'; Experience: 'I duel the eerie Baron across the abbey roof; he nearly takes my head, but I do not die.')
